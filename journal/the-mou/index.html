02 · The agreement

What the MOU with the Araku society means

57 farmers, six mandals, and what we actually pay and provide.

10 November 2026 · 4 min read

We have a signed MOU with the Araku Tribal Development and Sustainable Agriculture Society. It is how we work with 57 tribal farmers in six mandals: 35 in Araku Valley, 8 in Ananthagiri, 5 in Hukumpeta, and 3 each in Pedabayalu, Manchingiput and Dumbriguda.

Price. When a farmer tells us his cherries are ready, we note that day's coffee price. By the day he brings the coffee in, the price may have moved. We look at both days, take the higher one, and add one rupee for every kilogram of cherries. That is a small premium, and we say so plainly. It is paid on top of the best price of the two days, not the worst.

Time. Farmers are paid within seven working days.

Tools. Every farmer receives a tool kit and farm safety clothing.

Work. The cherries are processed, and the coffee is roasted, in Araku. That is paid employment for people from the same communities, and the roasting is done to our standard.

What we are planning, and have not started: for each farmer's daughter we intend to open a Sukanya Samriddhi account and pay the yearly deposit on her behalf, 2,500 rupees a year, so that she has a lump sum when she is grown, for education, marriage or whatever she chooses. It is a plan. We will tell you here when the first account is open.
